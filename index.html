About

I am a third year Ph.D. student in Civil and Environmental Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. My work lies at the intersection of transportation engineering, operations research, electric mobility, network science, and data analytics.

I study how uncertainty, traveler behavior, charging demand, infrastructure disruptions, and decentralized decisions affect transportation and energy systems. My goal is to develop methods that support more reliable, resilient, and efficient mobility systems.

Area 01 Stochastic Network Science Centrality and critical-node identification when network structure is uncertain. +
Research Problem

A stochastic network incorporates randomness and probability distributions into its structure or attributes—such as treating edge existence, capacities, flow demands, or travel times as random variables rather than fixed values. We study them because real-world complex systems like transportation grids, communication networks, and power infrastructure are inherently subject to unpredictable demand shifts, weather disruptions, and random failures. By explicitly modeling these uncertainties, researchers can quantify system reliability, evaluate resilience under extreme events, and design robust, risk-averse decision-making strategies that prevent fragile failures in dynamic environments.

Area 02 Transportation Modeling, Optimization & Policy Traveler behavior, resilient network design, and multimodal policy evaluation. +
Research Problem

A transportation system is an integrated network of physical infrastructure, vehicles, and mobility demands designed to move people and goods efficiently across space and time. We study these systems to maximize societal mobility, economic productivity, and network resilience by optimizing strategic infrastructure planning, such as solving facility location problems for EV charging stations or logistics hubs, and by understanding decentralized user behavior, like modeling user equilibrium to predict how self-interested route choices shape overall traffic flow. Ultimately, analyzing these mechanics allows planners to align system investments with actual human behavior to build efficient, robust transportation networks.

Area 03 EV Charging Behavior & Resilience Charging-demand heterogeneity, hazard exposure, and infrastructure reliability. +
Research Problem

A transportation electrification system couples power grids with mobility networks through electric vehicle infrastructure and dynamic driver charging behaviors. We study electric vehicle infrastructure and user charging patterns because mass adoption fundamentally transforms both traffic networks and power grids, creating unprecedented spatial-temporal electricity demand peaks that can strain local transformers and cause severe grid congestion if left unmanaged.
